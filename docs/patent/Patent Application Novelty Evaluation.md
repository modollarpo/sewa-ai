# **Patentability and Novelty Evaluation Report: Clinical Artificial Intelligence Platform for Risk-Controlled Radiological Triage**

## **Executive Legal and Technical Assessment**

This evaluation assesses the legal novelty, inventive step, and statutory patentability of the UK Patent Application filed by STOREGRILL INC LTD (Inventor: Dolapo Ariyo). The application is titled *"Clinical Artificial Intelligence Platform and Method for Distribution-Free Statistical Risk-Controlled Radiological Triage, Zero-Label Domain Adaptation, and Anatomically-Bounded Attribution"*. Formally filed under Section 14 of the UK Patents Act 1977, the specification claims priority under Section 5(2) and Article 4 of the Paris Convention from senior UK Patent Application No. GB2609706.3, filed on 25 April 2026\.  
The claimed subject matter defines a Software-as-a-Medical-Device (SaMD) computing platform and computer-implemented method engineered for time-critical emergency radiology workflows, including acute intracranial hemorrhage, stroke, pulmonary embolism, and pneumothorax. The platform addresses widespread clinical failures observed in first-generation commercial triage algorithms by combining six core technical integers:

> 1. **Unsupervised Zero-Label Adaptive Batch Normalization (AdaBN):** A domain adaptation subsystem that updates running mean (\\mu\_t) and variance (\\sigma\_t^2) feature statistics directly on unannotated target scanner image streams while maintaining convolutional weights, biases, and linear classification heads strictly frozen.  
> 2. **Relative Mahalanobis Distance (RMD) Out-of-Distribution Gating:** An out-of-distribution detector that computes background-relative Mahalanobis distances using Ledoit-Wolf trace shrinkage regularization (\\lambda\_{\\text{shrink}} \= 0.05) and Moore-Penrose pseudoinverses over penultimate latent feature representations.  
> 3. **Split Conformal Risk Control Safety Gate:** A distribution-free statistical risk bounding gate enforcing finite-sample expected bounds on the conditional False Negative Rate (E\[\\text{FNR}\_{\\text{cond}}\] \\le \\alpha, configured at \\alpha \= 0.05) among positive-class imaging studies under exchangeability, utilizing a calibrated quantile threshold (\\lambda) derived as the supremum threshold (\\lambda\_{\\text{sup}}) or a conservative operational threshold (\\lambda\_{\\text{op}} \\le \\lambda\_{\\text{sup}}).  
> 4. **Anatomically-Masked High-Resolution Class Activation Mapping (Masked HiResCAM):** A spatial attribution engine computing unpooled element-wise gradient-activation product feature maps multiplied by an automated parenchymal soft-tissue segmentation mask (0 to \+85 Hounsfield Units) to suppress high-attenuation cortical calvarial bone hyperdensities (+500 to \+2000 HU).  
> 5. **Physics-Monotonic Epistemic Uncertainty Quantification:** A dual uncertainty engine evaluating Monte Carlo dropout prediction variance under progressive physical acquisition perturbations (Gaussian radiation noise, motion blur) to verify that model uncertainty exhibits a monotonic response (\\rho \\ge 0.85) to image degradation.  
> 6. **Deterministic Clinical Routing & Cryptographic Database Governance:** An integrated governance subsystem that combines multi-gate safety signals and enforces a database-level foreign key constraint requiring cryptographic clinician digital verification (signedBy, signedAt) prior to final report release to Electronic Health Records.

Based on a comparative examination against commercial triage devices (Aidoc, Viz.ai, Avicenna.ai), statistical conformal prediction literature (Bates et al., Angelopoulos et al.), out-of-distribution metrics (Ren et al.), and explainability frameworks (Draelos & Carin), all independent claims (Claims 1, 15, 21, and 26\) meet the statutory threshold of novelty under Section 2 of the UK Patents Act 1977\. Furthermore, the application demonstrates an inventive step under Section 3 of the Act due to the structural co-design and functional synergy across the technical integers. The specification also defines patentable subject matter under Section 1(2) as it produces a technical contribution that controls physical scanning workflows and mitigates acquisition sensor noise.

## **Comprehensive Prior Art Landscape and Benchmark Analysis**

To evaluate novelty under Section 2 and inventive step under Section 3 of the Patents Act 1977, the subject claims must be benchmarked against five distinct categories of prior art across commercial devices, statistical learning frameworks, domain adaptation techniques, and explainability methods.  
Commercial clinical AI triage platforms (such as Aidoc, Viz.ai, and Avicenna.ai CINA-CHEST) process DICOM images using deep convolutional neural networks and flag positive emergency cases on Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS) worklists. These commercial devices operate using static fixed probability cutoffs (typically \\tau \\ge 0.50 or \\tau \\ge 0.70). When exposed to scanner hardware variations across hospital fleets—such as changes in slice thickness (0.625\\text{ mm} to 5.0\\text{ mm}), peak kilovoltage (kVp), or reconstructive kernels—these static classifiers suffer from distribution shift. Empirical testing on multi-center clinical cohorts demonstrates that static thresholds produce false positive rates as high as 20.07\\%, causing severe alert fatigue.  
Furthermore, prospective real-world clinical studies published in major radiological journals evaluate commercial AI triage deployments and show that commercial devices fail to provide statistically guaranteed false negative risk bounds. For instance, a 2024 prospective study evaluating a widely used commercial triage tool for intracranial hemorrhage detection on head CTs demonstrated no significant difference in radiologists' overall diagnostic accuracy (99.5\\% without AI vs 99.2\\% with AI) or report turnaround times (147.1\\text{ minutes} without AI vs 149.9\\text{ minutes} with AI). Commercial platforms lack distribution-free risk bounding, zero-label domain adaptation, and hard database sign-off constraints.  
In the academic domain, statistical frameworks for Risk-Controlling Prediction Sets (RCPS) and Conformal Risk Control (CRC) were established by Bates et al. (2021) and Angelopoulos et al. (2021, 2024a, 2024b). Conformal triage literature explores tuning binary classifiers to bound overall error rates. However, standard conformal prediction frameworks assume that calibration and test data are drawn from identical, exchangeable distributions. When deployed across disparate clinical sites, acquisition hardware variations violate exchangeability, degrading the coverage guarantees. The subject patent application addresses this failure mode by integrating test-time Zero-Label Adaptive Batch Normalization directly upstream of the conformal prediction gate to maintain activation exchangeability under scanner drift.  
Out-of-distribution (OOD) detection techniques employing Relative Mahalanobis Distance (RMD) were introduced by Ren et al. (2021) to contrast class-conditional distributions against an unconditioned background distribution. While RMD as a mathematical metric is known, executing RMD with Ledoit-Wolf trace shrinkage (0.05) over penultimate feature representations extracted *after* test-time AdaBN adaptation represents a distinct technical arrangement. This arrangement prevents acquisition hardware gain shifts from skewing background distance calculations.  
Adaptive Batch Normalization (AdaBN) was originally proposed by Li et al. (2016) and subsequently adapted across medical image segmentation literature. The subject application does not claim AdaBN in isolation. Instead, Claim 1(b) and Claim 7 incorporate AdaBN as an unlabelled warm-up stream module (momentum \\eta \= 0.1, batch size m \\approx 50\\text{--}100) operating with strictly frozen convolutional weights and frozen linear classification heads, functioning as an internal feature-standardization integer within a larger clinical pipeline.  
In explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), HiResCAM (Draelos & Carin, 2020\) computes unpooled element-wise gradient-activation products. Skull stripping and radiological Hounsfield Unit thresholding are standard preprocessing operations in neuroradiology. The subject application's Claim 21 combines unpooled HiResCAM activation maps with a dynamic parenchymal soft-tissue attenuation mask (0 to \+85 HU). This suppresses calvarial bone hyperdensity artifacts (+500 to \+2000 HU) and feeds the resulting filtered attribution map directly into Layer 4 (Attribution Coherence) of an automated 9-layer metacognition safety engine.

## **Detailed Claim-by-Claim Novelty and Inventive Step Evaluation**

### **Independent Method Claim 1 and System Claim 15**

Independent Claim 1 defines a computer-implemented method comprising eight sequential and functionally co-dependent operational steps (a) through (h). System Claim 15 recites corresponding physical computing hardware, including a hardware processor, medical imaging interface, deep neural network inference engine, zero-label AdaBN module, split conformal risk control module, explainability engine, and relational database enforcing sign-off constraints.  
To establish anticipation under Section 2 of the UK Patents Act 1977, a single prior art document must contain a clear and unambiguous disclosure of every technical integer recited in the claim. None of the cited references—including commercial patent portfolios (Aidoc, Viz.ai), academic conformal prediction literature (Bates et al., Angelopoulos et al.), out-of-distribution studies (Ren et al.), domain adaptation papers (Li et al.), or explainability publications (Draelos & Carin)—discloses the combined operational steps (a)–(h) within a single technical system. Commercial systems lack distribution-free statistical risk control, zero-label AdaBN domain adaptation, and database-level foreign key constraints. Statistical literature on conformal prediction fails to teach test-time AdaBN moment updating on unlabelled target streams combined with Anatomically-Masked HiResCAM and database-enforced sign-off gates. Consequently, Claim 1 and Claim 15 are novel under Section 2\.  
Inventive step under Section 3 of the Patents Act 1977 is evaluated using the structured four-step *Windsurfing / Pozzoli* inquiry:

> 1. **Identification of the Person Skilled in the Art and Common General Knowledge:** The skilled person is a multi-disciplinary team comprising a medical software engineer, a machine learning scientist specializing in medical computer vision, and a consultant emergency radiologist. The common general knowledge includes standard deep neural network architectures (EfficientNet, ResNet), standard DICOM/HL7/FHIR communication protocols, basic Grad-CAM, standard batch normalization, and basic statistical hypothesis testing.  
> 2. **Identification of the Inventive Concept:** The inventive concept resides in a multi-tier safety architecture wherein test-time domain adaptation (AdaBN) standardizes internal activation moments across scanner fleets to preserve data exchangeability, enabling a downstream split conformal prediction gate to enforce a strict conditional false negative risk ceiling (E\[\\text{FNR}\_{\\text{cond}}\] \\le \\alpha), while secondary OOD, epistemic, masked attribution, and database constraints contain out-of-distribution and hardware failure modes.  
> 3. **Identification of Differences Between Prior Art and the Inventive Concept:** Prior art commercial systems apply uncalibrated static probability cutoffs to un-adapted scanner inputs. Prior art statistical frameworks apply conformal prediction to raw features without controlling for scanner covariate shift, or utilize unmasked attribution maps that suffer from calvarial bone gradient artifacts.  
> 4. **Assessment of Obviousness:** Combining these technical integers is non-obvious to the skilled person because the sub-systems interact functionally. Without AdaBN pre-conditioning, scanner covariate shift breaks the exchangeability assumption required by Split Conformal Risk Control, causing the statistical safety guarantee to fail when deployed on external hardware. Without anatomical masking, HiResCAM attribution maps feed spurious calvarial bone gradients into downstream metacognitive plausibility checks. This functional interdependence produces a synergistic technical effect that satisfies Section 3 of the Act.

### **Dependent Claims Analysis**

Claim 2 recites the quantile threshold solver selecting operational probability threshold \\lambda as the supremum threshold \\lambda\_{\\text{sup}} satisfying:  
\\lambda\_{\\text{sup}} \= \\sup \\left\\{ \\lambda' \\in \[0, 1\] : \\left(\\frac{n\_{\\text{pos}}}{n\_{\\text{pos}} \+ 1}\\right) R\_n(\\lambda') \+ \\frac{1}{n\_{\\text{pos}} \+ 1} \\le \\alpha \\right\\}  
or as an operational threshold strictly less than said supremum (\\lambda\_{\\text{op}} \\le \\lambda\_{\\text{sup}}). Claim 3 specifies configuring \\alpha \= 0.05 (bounding conditional FNR \\le 5.0\\%). Claim 5 extends this to subtype-specific risk thresholds for intraparenchymal, epidural, subdural, subarachnoid, and intraventricular hemorrhages. While the mathematical basis of RCPS was published by Bates et al. (2021), applying a conditional FNR loss:  
R\_n(\\lambda') \= \\frac{1}{n\_{\\text{pos}}} \\sum\_{i: Y\_i=1} I(S(X\_i) \< \\lambda')  
paired with a dual-threshold operational margin (\\lambda\_{\\text{op}} \= 0.0298 vs \\lambda\_{\\text{sup}} \= 0.0753) and subtype-specific multi-head calibration represents a novel and non-obvious application in acute radiology triage. In empirical testing on the CQ500 cohort (N=386 test scans), operating at \\lambda\_{\\text{op}} \= 0.0298 achieved an observed test conditional FNR of 3.70\\% (6/162), successfully meeting the pre-registered 5.0\\% ceiling.  
Claim 7 specifies updating running channel mean (\\mu\_{t,c}^{(l)}) and variance ((\\sigma\_{t,c}^{(l)})^2) statistics via an unlabelled warm-up mini-batch (m \\approx 50\\text{--}100 scans) with exponential moving momentum \\eta \= 0.1, while keeping all convolutional filter weights (W^{(l)}) and linear classification weights (W\_{\\text{head}}) strictly frozen. Although AdaBN is known in general computer vision, its inclusion in Claim 1 and Claim 7 as a zero-label, non-backpropagating pre-conditioning engine specifically designed to align incoming scanner activation moments prior to evaluating a conformal risk control gate provides non-obvious technical synergy. As disclosed in the specification, test-time AdaBN improved cross-center study AUROC on the CQ500 cohort from 0.7491 to 0.7596 without requiring target labels or gradient updates, stabilizing downstream statistical gates against hardware drift.  
Claim 8 specifies computing RMD as:  
\\text{RMD}(x) \= \\min\_{c} \\left( (z(x) \- \\mu\_c)^T \\mathbf{\\Sigma}\_{\\text{shrunk}}^{-1} (z(x) \- \\mu\_c) \\right) \- (z(x) \- \\mu\_0)^T \\mathbf{\\Sigma}\_{0, \\text{shrunk}}^{-1} (z(x) \- \\mu\_0)  
using Ledoit-Wolf trace shrinkage (\\lambda\_{\\text{shrink}} \= 0.05) and Moore-Penrose pseudoinverses. Although Ren et al. (2021) disclosed the basic RMD formula, executing this metric on penultimate feature vectors extracted *after* test-time AdaBN adaptation prevents hardware acquisition parameters (slice thickness, reconstructive filter) from skewing the background distribution \\mathcal{N}(\\mu\_0, \\mathbf{\\Sigma}\_0). In CQ500 error detection, this specific arrangement improved OOD detection AUROC from 0.5256 (standard Mahalanobis) to 0.5798.  
Claim 21 recites an independent method for generating a bone-artifact-suppressed attribution map by:

> 1. Computing unpooled element-wise gradient-activation products (HiResCAM):

> H\_c(u, v) \= \\sum\_{k} \\left( \\frac{\\partial Y\_c}{\\partial A\_k(u, v)} \\cdot A\_k(u, v) \\right)

> 1. Generating a parenchymal soft-tissue mask M\_{\\text{brain}}(u, v) \\in \\{0, 1\\} by thresholding CT voxels to 0 to \+85 HU (capturing brain tissue \[20\\text{--}40 HU\], CSF \[0\\text{--}15 HU\], and acute hematoma \[50\\text{--}85 HU\]).  
> 2. Executing element-wise multiplication:

A\_{\\text{final}}(u, v) \= \\text{ReLU}(H\_c(u, v)) \\odot M\_{\\text{brain}}(u, v)  
thereby suppressing false-positive gradient activation along cortical calvarial bone (+500 to \+2000 HU). Claim 21 and its dependent claims (Claims 23–25) possess independent novelty and inventive step. Standard Grad-CAM averages gradients over spatial dimensions, causing dense calvarial bone to capture gradient energy and yielding an 11.20\\% pointing hit rate on head CTs. By combining spatial HiResCAM with dynamic Hounsfield soft-tissue segmentation, Masked HiResCAM increases pointing-game hit rates to 43.83\\% overall across 6,283 expert bounding box annotations, and to 62.16\\% on chronic subdural hematomas. This technical improvement in spatial attribution fidelity satisfies Section 3 of the Patents Act 1977\.  
Claim 9 monitors epistemic variance (\\sigma\_{\\text{epi}}^2) across N=20 Monte Carlo dropout forward passes. It verifies that uncertainty responds monotonically (Spearman \\rho \\ge 0.85) to simulated or detected image acquisition degradation (radiation photon noise, motion blur), automatically escalating the scan to mandatory human review if this expected response fails. Utilizing MC dropout as a physics-grounded sensor integrity check—where a failure of uncertainty monotonicity triggers automated clinical escalation—is a novel operational feature. Empirical validation on N=300 held-out slices demonstrated a Spearman rank correlation of \\rho \= 0.9000 (p \= 0.0374) across progressive noise levels (\\sigma \\in \\{0.00, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35\\}), establishing a reliable check for image degradation.  
Claim 1(h) and Claim 15 require that prioritised triage alerts be dispatched to medical display worklists, but final diagnostic report delivery to Electronic Health Records (EHR) is physically blocked at the relational database level absent cryptographic consultant radiologist digital verification (signedBy, signedAt). Conventional clinical software enforces user authorization at the application layer. Moving this gate to a database-level foreign key constraint provides cryptographic containment, preventing unverified AI outputs from polluting clinical EHRs even in the event of application layer bypass.

## **Technical Synergy, Functional Interdependence, and Defense-in-Depth**

Claims 22 and 26 specifically recite the structural co-design and functional synergy across the platform's layers. Under European Patent Office (EPO) Case Law (G 1/15) and UKIPO guidelines, a combination of known integers is patentable if the integers interact to produce a novel technical result that exceeds the sum of their individual effects.  
The technical integers of the platform exhibit structural functional coupling across four primary operational boundaries:  
First, feature standardization directly pre-conditions statistical risk control. Split Conformal Risk Control relies on the assumption that calibration data D\_{\\text{cal}} and operational target data D\_{\\text{test}} are exchangeable. In heterogeneous clinical environments, scanner hardware variations introduce channel-wise activation shifts that violate exchangeability. Zero-Label AdaBN standardizes internal activation statistics on target scanner feeds while preserving frozen weights, mitigating hardware-induced covariate drift *before* threshold evaluation by the conformal prediction gate.  
Second, latent manifold regularization stabilizes out-of-distribution gating. Relative Mahalanobis Distance calculates background-relative distances (M(z(x); \\mu\_0, \\mathbf{\\Sigma}\_0)) on penultimate feature vectors z(x). Without upstream AdaBN, hardware variations introduce non-pathological shifts into the background distance, artificially inflating OOD scores and causing false escalations. Executing AdaBN in upstream layers regularizes the latent space prior to covariance estimation, ensuring that trace-shrunk precision matrices isolate true pathological anomalies rather than hardware noise.  
Third, spatial attribution artifact suppression enables metacognitive verification. Unmasked activation maps on head CTs suffer from gradient hijacking caused by high-attenuation calvarial bone (+500 to \+2000 HU). Anatomically-Masked HiResCAM removes these bone artifacts via an intracranial soft-tissue mask (0 to \+85 HU). Crucially, the resulting attribution map is ingested by Layer 4 (Attribution Coherence) of the automated 9-layer metacognition safety engine, enabling downstream verification of whether model focus aligns with parenchymal tissue and preventing cranial hyperdensities from triggering spurious triage rejections.  
Fourth, the architecture establishes complementary failure-mode partitioning (defense-in-depth). The retrospective multi-gate safety audit disclosed in §7.3 and §7.4 demonstrates the non-redundant partitioning of failure boundaries. In an evaluation of 6 false-negative studies that scored below the conformal threshold (\\lambda\_{\\text{op}} \= 0.0298), the secondary Relative Mahalanobis OOD gate intercepted 2 of the 6 cases (CQ500-CT-415, \\text{RMD} \= 55.54; CQ500-CT-43, \\text{RMD} \= 64.31), escalating them for human review due to anomalous feature distributions. The remaining 4 false negatives were not flagged by secondary gates, supporting the architectural requirement of mandatory radiologist sign-off prior to diagnostic release (Claim 1(h)).

## **Assessment of Statutory Exclusions (Section 1(2) UK Patents Act 1977\)**

Under Section 1(2) of the UK Patents Act 1977, a patent shall not be granted for a computer program "as such" or a mathematical method "as such". The UK Intellectual Property Office (UKIPO) assesses patentability using the four-step *Aerotel/Macrossan* framework, guided by the *AT\&T / Signify* signposts for technical contribution.  
In step one, the claims are properly construed. Claim 1 and Claim 15 define a Software-as-a-Medical-Device (SaMD) system that ingests physical DICOM sensor signals from imaging scanners, standardizes activation statistics, evaluates distribution-free statistical safety bounds, generates bone-suppressed spatial saliency maps, controls PACS priority routing, and blocks report delivery via database constraints.  
In step two, the actual contribution is identified. The contribution is an integrated clinical AI architecture that combines target domain normalization, distribution-free false negative risk bounding, anatomical artifact suppression, and database-level sign-off enforcement to enable safe emergency triage across heterogeneous hospital hardware fleets.  
In step three, it is evaluated whether the contribution falls solely within excluded subject matter. The contribution does not reside solely in a mathematical algorithm (such as conformal prediction or Mahalanobis distance) or a computer program "as such". It applies these algorithms to process physical sensor signals (radiological Hounsfield Unit attenuation measurements) and controls external physical workflows (PACS worklist prioritization and emergency alert dispatch).  
In step four, the technical nature of the contribution is confirmed under the *AT\&T / Signify* signposts:

> * **Signpost 1 (Technical Effect Outside the Computer):** The claimed system controls physical clinical workflows, accelerating door-to-treatment time for life-threatening conditions (e.g., stroke, intracranial hemorrhage) and altering the operation of connected PACS worklists and emergency notification systems.  
> * **Signpost 2 (Technical Effect at the System Architecture Level):** Zero-Label AdaBN and Anatomically-Masked HiResCAM solve technical hardware problems—specifically scanner-induced activation shift and calvarial bone gradient hijacking in CT imaging sensors.  
> * **Signpost 4 (Processing Physical Data):** The input data consists of physical measurements of tissue x-ray attenuation (Hounsfield Units) acquired by physical CT/MRI scanners.

Consequently, the subject matter claimed in the application is patentable subject matter under Section 1(2) of the Patents Act 1977\.

## **Overall Conclusions and Actionable Guidance**

The UK Patent Application filed by STOREGRILL INC LTD defines a novel, inventive, and patentable SaMD triage architecture under the UK Patents Act 1977 and European Patent Convention guidelines. The key conclusions and prosecution guidance are as follows:  
First, all independent claims (Claims 1, 15, 21, and 26\) satisfy Section 2 novelty because no prior art publication or commercial device discloses the complete multi-tier combination of zero-label domain adaptation, split conformal risk control, background-relative OOD gating, anatomically-masked attribution, and database-enforced sign-off constraints.  
Second, the claims satisfy Section 3 inventive step due to the functional pre-conditioning and technical synergy between the integers. Upstream AdaBN standardizes activation distributions to preserve the exchangeability required by downstream Split Conformal Risk Control and regularizes the latent space for Relative Mahalanobis Distance gating. Anatomically-Masked HiResCAM removes calvarial bone gradient artifacts before attribution maps are evaluated by the metacognition safety engine.  
Third, during prosecution before the UKIPO and EPO, applicants should maintain the clear priority allocation defined in Section 3 of the specification, attributing foundational deployment and DICOM ingestion features to the senior priority date (25 April 2026\) while anchoring later mathematical formulations to the October 2026 filing date.
